Global Strategy · Market Access · Working Prototype

One playbook, many markets: find the real bottleneck in each one before sequencing the next launch

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  View on GitHub

"Global" usually means one launch playbook copied market by market. In practice every launch runs through the same four functions — evidence, regulatory, market access, and commercial execution — and what changes is which one is the bottleneck. This tool scores a fictional patient program's readiness across 13 markets, names the binding constraint in each, and sequences the next launches by opportunity and readiness, with the weights out in the open.

What this method learned
1
Nine launches don't predict the tenthMarkets that share a regulatory framework inherit the template. Markets outside it, even mature ones, need their own route — that's often why the most "obvious" markets are the ones missing.
2
Funded access isn't adoptionA reimbursement listing removes one barrier. Clinician workflow, awareness and integration decide whether anyone uses it.
3
Rank by the weakest linkAveraging four functions hides the one that will stall the launch. Readiness here is the minimum, and the tool names it.
4
Hypothesize, validate, executeOutside-in scores are a hypothesis. The first job in a real role is confirming them against internal data before acting on them.
